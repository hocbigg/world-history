Advanced Topics

  • Regional Histories in Global Perspective - Explores individual world regions in depth while situating their local political and cultural developments within wider global historical currents.
  • Trans-Regional and Oceanic Histories - Focuses on maritime basins and overland conduits that dissolve nation-state borders through sustained commercial, demographic, and cultural exchange.
  • Global Environmental and Economic History - Analyzes the material engines of history, including long-term ecological transformations, energy transitions, commodity circuits, and the roots of global inequality.
  • Empires, Imperialism, and Decolonization - Examines comparative imperial structures, anti-colonial revolutions, Great Power rivalries, and the ideological clashes that formed the post-colonial order.
  • Global Science, Technology, and Material Culture - Investigates the worldwide circulation of scientific ideas, cartographic power, material artifacts, and the historical impact of epidemic diseases.

Regional Histories in Global Perspective

This track provides area-specific historical depth across major non-Western civilizations, balancing local agency and internal institutions against trans-regional pressures.

Sub-Saharan Africa: General History of Africa (UNESCO) - A foundational multi-volume open-access corpus authored primarily by African scholars to decenter colonial narratives and recover continental history from antiquity to the post-colonial period.

East Asia: ChinaX: China's Past, Present and Future (Harvard University / edX) - An advanced multi-part open course exploring Chinese dynastic statecraft, philosophical paradigms, bureaucratic structures, and modern transformations in comparative perspective.

The Middle East and Islamic World: A History of the Modern Middle East (William L. Cleveland and Martin Bunton) - An authoritative advanced text analyzing Ottoman and Qajar administrative transformations, European colonial mandates, nationalist movements, and modern state formation.

Latin America and the Caribbean: Born in Blood and Fire: A Concise History of Latin America (John Charles Chasteen) - An incisive narrative connecting the colonial encounter, racial caste hierarchies, trans-Atlantic independence movements, and modern populist revolutions.

South Asia: A Concise History of Modern India (Barbara D. Metcalf and Thomas R. Metcalf) - An essential study examining the decline of the Mughal Empire, British imperial statecraft, anti-colonial resistance, and the complex historical roots of religious and national identities.

Trans-Regional and Oceanic Histories

This track examines geographic zones defined by water and land corridors, highlighting how cross-cultural mobility and maritime networks shaped global interconnectedness.

The Atlantic World: The Atlantic World: Europeans, Africans, Indians and Their Shared History, 1400–1900 (Thomas Benjamin) - A comprehensive spatial history detailing how coerced labor systems, triangular trade, and imperial rivalries generated a interconnected ocean basin.

The Indian Ocean Arena: The Indian Ocean (Michael N. Pearson) - A pioneer study that centers the ocean itself to analyze monsoon trade routes, cosmopolitan coastal enclaves, and inter-Asian commercial networks from antiquity through European arrival.

The Silk Roads and Inner Asia: The Silk Roads: A New History of the World (Peter Frankopan) - A sweeping trans-Eurasian narrative demonstrating how overland trading networks through Central Asia mediated the transmission of global capital, religious faiths, and geopolitical power.

The Pacific Basin: The Great Ocean: Pacific Worlds from Captain Cook to the Gold Rush (David Igler) - An advanced environmental and maritime history examining the rapid integration of Indigenous Pacific societies, marine exploitation, and global trade corridors in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Global Environmental and Economic History

This track investigates the material underpinnings of human societies, exploring ecological constraints, energy regimes, and the structural causes of global economic divergence.

The Great Divergence: The Great Divergence: China, Europe, and the Making of the Modern World Economy (Kenneth Pomeranz) - A landmark comparative work analyzing why sustained industrial development began in Northwest Europe rather than wealthy core regions of East Asia.

Ecological Imperialism: Ecological Imperialism: The Biological Expansion of Europe, 900–1900 (Alfred W. Crosby) - A seminal environmental analysis explaining how European animals, plants, and pathogens facilitated the conquest and settlement of temperate zones worldwide.

The Anthropocene: The Great Acceleration: An Environmental History of the Anthropocene since 1945 (J. R. McNeill and Peter Engelke) - An incisive study detailing the dramatic post-WWII acceleration of fossil fuel exploitation, urbanization, carbon emissions, and planetary biosphere alterations.

Global Commodity Chains: Empire of Cotton: A Global History (Sven Beckert) - A masterclass in global commodity history illustrating how coercive labor, imperial state power, and industrial manufacturing created modern global capitalism.

Empires, Imperialism, and Decolonization

This track explores the mechanics of imperial expansion, governance of multiethnic subject populations, revolutionary ruptures, and the collapse of empires in the modern era.

Comparative Imperial Statecraft: Empires in World History: Power and the Politics of Difference (Jane Burbank and Frederick Cooper) - A wide-ranging comparative analysis of how ancient, Eurasian, and modern maritime empires accommodated, manipulated, and enforced political difference.

The Age of Atlantic Revolutions: Revolutions in the Atlantic World: A Comparative History (Wim Klooster) - A comparative study of the structural causes and ideological connections uniting the American, French, Haitian, and Spanish-American revolutions.

The Global Cold War: The Global Cold War: Third World Interventions and the Making of Our Times (Odd Arne Westad) - A transformative global reassessment showing how ideological superpower interventions fueled post-colonial conflicts across Africa, Asia, and Latin America.

Great Power Dynamics: The Rise and Fall of the Great Powers (Paul Kennedy) - An influential strategic and economic analysis examining the interaction between economic productivity, military overexpansion, and imperial decline over five centuries.

Global Science, Technology, and Material Culture

This track analyzes how human societies generated, circulated, and applied knowledge, artifacts, and medical practices across cultural and geographic boundaries.

History of Global Science: Science and Technology in World History: An Introduction (James E. McClellan III and Harold Dorn) - A comprehensive global examination contrasting state-directed practical engineering with theoretical science across ancient civilizations, the Islamic world, China, and the industrial West.

Historical Cartography: A History of the World in Twelve Maps (Jerry Brotton) - A critical spatial study demonstrating how world maps from antiquity to Google Earth functioned as ideological instruments of empire, faith, and political dominance.

Material Culture and Artifacts: A History of the World in 100 Objects (Neil MacGregor / BBC Radio 4) - An audio masterclass exploring global human history, economic exchange, and cultural meaning through the close reading of material artifacts.

History of Epidemics and Public Health: Epidemics in Western Society Since 1600 (Open Yale Courses / Frank M. Snowden) - A complete university lecture series investigating how infectious diseases have shaped warfare, urban design, religious attitudes, and modern state capacity.
